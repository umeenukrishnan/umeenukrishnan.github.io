I'm U. Meenu Krishnan, a researcher who firmly believes that you should either love what you do, or change it — because passion fuels purpose, and purpose drives excellence.

I am currently working as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Johns Hopkins University, where my research focuses on Evolutionary Deep Neural Networks (EDNN). I use EDNN to solve multi-physics problems in solid mechanics — coupled fracture, heat conduction, and wave propagation — combining the strengths of physics-based modeling and machine learning.

My research journey began in January 2019 at the Computational Mechanics Lab, IIT Roorkee, where I worked on phase field fracture and topology optimization — developing computationally efficient algorithms with mesh adaptivity, automatic time stepping, and parallel HPC techniques.

Outside the lab, I love drawing, reading, and simply sitting in peace. These quiet moments help me recharge and often inspire fresh ideas in both life and research.

Research Projects

Key areas of investigation spanning computational modeling, optimization, and data-driven methods.

Phase Field Fracture

A powerful framework for simulating crack propagation in materials without explicit crack tracking algorithms. Enhanced with mesh adaptivity and auto time-stepping.

Topology Optimization

Mathematical approach to find optimal material layouts under fracture constraints and manufacturing considerations. Targets lightweight, high-strength structures.

SIMP Phase Field 3D Printing MPI

Evolutionary Deep Neural Networks

Using EDNN to solve multi-physics problems — coupled fracture, heat conduction, and wave propagation — bridging physics-based modeling and machine learning.
